## 13.1 Assigning bands to colours

A screen has three colours: red, green, blue. Sentinel-2 has 13 bands. A <Term id="composite">colour composite</Term> picks three bands and assigns them to those colours. When invisible bands (near-infrared, SWIR) are put on screen we get a <Term id="false-colour">false-colour composite</Term>.

| Composite (R-G-B) | Common name | Quick reading |
|---|---|---|
| 4-3-2 | True colour | As the eye sees; water dark, towns light grey |
| 8-4-3 | NIR false colour | Vegetation red; the healthier and denser, the redder |
| 11-8-2 | Agriculture | Crops bright green, bare soil brown, water black |
| 12-11-4 | SWIR urban | Concrete and metal roofs stand out; thin smoke almost transparent |

<Note kind="key">

**Colour is not a property of the feature.** The same rice field is green in 4-3-2, red in 8-4-3 and bright green in 11-8-2. Even within one composite the <Term id="stretch">stretch</Term> changes colours: a per-scene 2–98 % stretch makes two very different scenes look equally contrasted. When you show an image, always state the band combination and the stretch.

</Note>

## 13.2 The nine interpretation keys

Image interpreters do not only look at colour. Since the days of aerial photographs, the cues the eye uses to recognise features have been systematised as <Term id="interpretation-key">interpretation keys</Term> (Olson, 1960). Each key in Figure 13.2 comes with a real example and the spectral measurements at the pin.

<div class="worked">

#### Example: something that looks like water but is not

At the foot of Cam Mountain (An Giang) there is a very uniform blue-purple rectangle. In true colour it looks like a lake.

1. Measure the spectrum in a circle of radius 30 pixels in the middle: MNDWI = **−0.58**, NDVI = 0.22. Water has positive MNDWI (Chapter 11); the Han River in Da Nang has MNDWI = +0.49.
2. Pattern: the block is divided into a regular grid with straight internal tracks. Association: next to a settlement, on flat land.
3. Conclusion (**interpretation**): it is most likely a solar farm. Panels absorb visible light strongly, so they look dark like water, but their SWIR reflectance differs from water.

</div>

<Note kind="key">

The eye is easily fooled by tone and colour. Check an interpretation with spectral measurements (NDVI, MNDWI) and the shape, pattern and association keys.

</Note>

In the Ca Mau scene, 27.3 % of pixels have MNDWI > 0 (flooded ponds) and 35.7 % have NDVI > 0.6 (dense vegetation). The two alternate in parallel strips at right angles to the natural creeks: the pattern of shrimp farming mixed with mangroves. This is an **interpretation** from pattern and spectra; separating shrimp–mangrove from other pond types needs field data or finer imagery.

<Depth level="advanced" title="Common traps">

- **Automatic stretches mislead.** A 2–98 % stretch makes every scene look equally contrasted. To compare brightness between scenes, use the same fixed stretch (as in Chapter 12).
- **Terrain shading in L2A.** In the An Giang scene, forest NIR brightness correlates **negatively** with illumination computed from SRTM for the scene's sun angles (r = −0.47): shaded slopes are not darker as expected. The cause is not verified; one possibility is Sen2Cor's terrain correction. In mountains, do not rely on terrain shading in L2A images to infer slope direction.
- **Mixed pixels.** At 10 m a 15 m wide canal covers one or two pixels and mixes with its banks (Chapter 9). The bright winding lines in Ca Mau have negative MNDWI: they are most likely canal banks with roads and houses, not open water.
- **Date.** An Giang rice can be water, green crop or bare soil depending on the week (Chapter 11). Always put the acquisition date next to an interpretation.

</Depth>

<Note kind="key">

An interpretation comes with three facts: the band combination, the stretch and the acquisition date. Without any one of them, nobody else can check it.

</Note>
